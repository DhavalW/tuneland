1 · Name your world

This is what fans see on the way in.

2 · Add your songs

Upload an audio file (mp3, m4a, wav · up to 25 MB). It is stored on your own Cloudflare, played inside your world, and its loudness shapes your terrain: loud parts raise ridges, quiet parts sink into water valleys. While it uploads, your browser listens to it once to measure that shape.

Only upload music you have the rights to. You are hosting these files on your own Cloudflare account, in public. That means your own songs, or songs you have written permission to host and share. Covers, remixes with uncleared samples, and other people's tracks can get your Cloudflare and GitHub accounts suspended and can expose you to copyright claims. You are responsible for what your world serves.

3 · Shape your world

Your songs are the terrain. Pick how they lay out, order them, and give each one a color. Glowing time markers cross the land every 30 seconds of each song, so fans always know how deep into a track they are standing. The corner arenas stay neutral ground.

4 · Bury loot

Tap the map to pick a spot (the map is your whole world, top-down; darker is lower ground, blue is water). Fans dig it up and get whatever you put inside: a link, a code, a note. Limited drops disappear after that many finds.

No spot picked yet. Tap the map.

Scarcity works by count, by time, or both: "first 50 fans", "release weekend only", or "first 50 fans during release weekend".

5 · Join the Tuneland map

Registering puts your world in the public directory so fans can find it and other artists can link to you. Do this after naming your world.

6 · Link your corners

Pick one of your corners and another registered world. If they say yes, that corner becomes a shared arena: fans walk straight from your land into theirs, and you can both bury loot there.
